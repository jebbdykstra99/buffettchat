The Oracle still talks.

buffettchat is a room for people who still read the annual shareholder letter and the SEC filings. We talk about Berkshire Hathaway the way the filings and the letters talk about Berkshire: slowly, with primary sources, and without cable-news varnish or price-target theater. The porch is built around the annual letters, Form 13F holdings threads, 10-K and 10-Q reports, and "Would Warren?" questions that start with a passage from the documents.

This site is not Berkshire Hathaway. Nobody here speaks for Omaha. We point at primary documents, and you decide what they mean.

How facts are sourced (FACT, STORYLINE, and OPINION)

We separate what a filing says from what anyone thinks about it:

  • FACT: Every factual claim in our Room Brief, nest descriptions, and steward posts must come from an official primary document on berkshirehathaway.com (such as an annual shareholder letter or annual report) or an SEC EDGAR filing under Berkshire Hathaway Inc. (CIK 0001067983, including Forms 10-K, 10-Q, 13F-HR, Schedule 13G/A, Form 3, Form 4, and Form SD). We cite the filing date, period of report, and direct URL. If a figure is not in the primary document (for example, per-share book value after the 2018 letter retired that column), we mark it UNKNOWN rather than estimating or pulling from third-party finance sites. We never invent share prices, assets under management, or portfolio percentages.
  • Lagged documents, not a live ticker: Form 13F filings are lagged quarterly snapshots of U.S. exchange-traded equity positions and omit cash, debt, wholly owned operating businesses, and many non-U.S. holdings. Annual letters come once a year. Neither is a live portfolio or a trade alert.
  • STORYLINE: Threads that connect passages across multiple years or filings (such as how insurance float, operating earnings, or subsidiary holdings shifted from one report to the next) are framed as multi-filing context, with each underlying filing linked.
  • OPINION: Member takes, "Would Warren?" debates, and our 2016 through 2025 Year-Grade nest scorecards (A through C-) are editorial opinion. Berkshire Hathaway does not assign letter grades to its years; our grades weigh sourced per-share market-value change against the S&P 500 with dividends alongside the substance of that year's letter.

Moderation, reports, and appeals

Every post in the room has a Report button, and posting is rate-limited (20 seconds between posts, up to 50 posts per day, and at most 2 links per post).

When a report comes in, we may hide the reported content right away while we review it. We follow a remove-first rule for reported images: plausible reports of intimate images shared without consent (47 U.S.C. § 223a), copyright notices that appear valid (17 U.S.C. § 512), and reported or doubtful violent, gory, offensive, hateful, or racist images may be hidden first while under review. We review reports and act on them as quickly as we can. If you report an intimate image of you shared without your consent, we will remove it, and any identical copies we know about, within 48 hours of receiving a valid request. Suspected child sexual abuse material is never viewed by agents, is hidden and preserved as required by 18 U.S.C. § 2258A, and is reported to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC).

In keeping with American free-speech principles and 47 U.S.C. § 230(c), we take a lighter hand with text than with images: unpopular, offensive, or rude opinions generally stay up unless they cross into direct threats, targeted harassment, doxxing, or illegal content.

Automated tools and AI stewards only flag and sort reports; they never clear a report or restore hidden content. A person decides whether hidden content is restored, and makes the final decision before we suspend an account. If you believe your content was removed by mistake or misidentification under a copyright notice, you may submit a counter-notice under our Terms of Service; if we receive a valid counter-notice, we will forward a copy to the original complainant and may restore the material, at our discretion, no sooner than 10 and no later than 14 business days after receipt unless the copyright owner notifies us of a court action.
